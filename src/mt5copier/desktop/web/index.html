How copying works

One account leads and the rest follow. Every trade the master makes — each open, change and close — is copied to every enabled slave, sized by that slave's own settings. A profile can instead trade directly: then there is no master terminal — the Trading page's order ticket leads, and what it places goes to the slaves the same way.

Everything starts as a dry run: the copier works out every copy and logs it, but sends nothing. Orders only become real when the profile goes live, on Settings — and going live asks you to type LIVE.

Starting from nothing

  1. Find your terminals. On Terminals, Rescan lists every MT5 terminal running on this machine; a cTrader account joins through cTrader login…. Drag a card up into MASTER or SLAVES to give it a role.
  2. Put accounts on them. A card's log in link signs a terminal into a saved account; a terminal already logged in inside MT5 is picked up as it is. One account runs on one terminal at a time.
  3. Tune each terminal on its own page. Click a card: risk sizing, breakeven, symbol decoration and the rest live there — each terminal keeps its own.
  4. Check the copier's switches on Settings. What the profile is, dry run or live, timings. Stay in the dry run until the Log and History show the copies you expect.
  5. Then go live — and keep the arrangement. Save it on Setups under a name, so this exact wiring can be put back later with one click.

The pages

Terminals
every terminal as a card in three zones — the master, the slaves, and the detected ones not yet used. Drag to assign, right-click for actions, click a card for its own page.
Trading
the direct-trading ticket: place, modify, partly close or flatten — and every enabled slave copies it. Greyed out unless the profile trades directly.
Guards
rules that watch an account — daily loss, drawdown, lot caps and the rest — and decline, shrink or stop trades before a limit is reached. Rules belong to the account, and follow it to any terminal, in any profile.
News
the week's Forex Factory releases, and what the news rule will do about each one — close or breakeven copies minutes before the release, if the rule is on.
History
one card per copied trade with every step under it: placed, filled, changed, closed, and anything a guard did along the way. Money stays in the terminal — no P/L here, on purpose.
Setups
saved arrangements: who copies from whom — or, in a direct-trading profile, which slaves trade — each terminal's account and settings. A setup loads into a profile of the mode it was saved in, and loading never turns live trading on by itself.
Settings
the copier's own switches, for every terminal: what the profile is, dry run or live, connection timings. A save lands whole or not at all. The timings are the machine's and shared by every profile; the rest is the profile's own. The symbol catalog — which asset class each symbol is, for guard rules that care — also lives here, shared by every profile.

Always on screen

Profile pill
profiles are separate copiers — each its own window, terminals and settings. The pill (top left) opens another, or makes, renames and deletes them. What describes the account or the machine is shared between them: account names, guard rules and their memory, the cTrader login, connection timings.
Halt all
stops this profile's copying at once; nothing more is sent until the same button resumes it.
Status bar
worker and feed health, what is being copied, and a DRY RUN badge whenever nothing real is sent.
Log
the Log button raises a dock under every page — the copier's log and day book merged, narrowed to one terminal, problems only, or a word.

Words the app uses

dry run
the copier does everything but send: each would-be copy is worked out and logged, so the wiring can be judged safely.
claimed
a terminal in use by one profile window is claimed, so a second window cannot drive it too. “In use elsewhere” on a card means exactly that; a stale claim clears itself within seconds.
given up
a copy left open but no longer managed — nothing will close or change it. Only unassigning or disabling a slave gives copies up; editing settings never does.
breakeven
the stop moved to the entry plus costs once a trade is far enough ahead, so it can no longer lose.
buffer
a guard acts at its buffer, deliberately short of the firm's hard limit — the tick mark on each rule's bar.
setup vs profile
a setup is a saved arrangement inside this profile; a profile is a whole separate copier with a window of its own.
